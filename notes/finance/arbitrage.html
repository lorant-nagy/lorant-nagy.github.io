Arbitrage

Market notation

π̄ = (π⁰, π) ∈ [0,∞)d+1,   π⁰ = 1;
S̄ = (S⁰, S),   Sⁱ : (Ω,ℱ,P) → [0,∞);
S⁰ ≡ 1+r,   r > −1,   ξ̄ = (ξ⁰,ξ) ∈ ℝd+1.

Cost: π̄·ξ̄; payoff: S̄·ξ̄. Negative holdings allow borrowing and short sales; no transaction costs. Under NA, discard zero-price assets and take πⁱ > 0.

Definition · Arbitrage

π̄·ξ̄ ≤ 0,   S̄·ξ̄ ≥ 0 P-a.s.,
P(S̄·ξ̄ > 0) > 0.

Lemma · Risky-asset criterion

Arbitrage exists iff some ξ ∈ ℝd satisfies

ξ·S ≥ (1+r)ξ·π   P-a.s.,
P(ξ·S > (1+r)ξ·π) > 0.

Lemma · Zero-cost and negative-cost portfolios

  • Arbitrage exists iff a zero-cost arbitrage exists.
  • π̄·η̄ < 0 and S̄·η̄ ≥ 0 P-a.s. imply arbitrage.
  • Arbitrage need not imply the existence of such a negative-cost portfolio.

Definition · Risk-neutral (martingale) measure

πⁱ = 𝔼*[Sⁱ/(1+r)],   i = 0,…,d.
𝒫 ≔ {P* : P* ∼ P,   P* risk-neutral}.

P* ∼ P: same null sets, equivalently dP*/dP > 0 P-a.s.

Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing

No arbitrage ⇔ 𝒫 ≠ ∅.

In this case some P* ∈ 𝒫 has bounded density dP*/dP.

Discounted net gains

Yⁱ ≔ Sⁱ/(1+r) − πⁱ,   i = 1,…,d.

Lemma.

NA ⇔ ∀ξ ∈ ℝd:
[ξ·Y ≥ 0 P-a.s. ⇒ ξ·Y = 0 P-a.s.].

Lemma.

P* risk-neutral ⇔ 𝔼*[Y] = 0.

Numéraire

A strictly positive asset used as the unit of account. The bond gives Sⁱ/(1+r). Taking asset 1, with π¹ > 0, S¹ > 0 P-a.s., gives prices πⁱ/π¹, Sⁱ/S¹ and pricing measures

𝒫̃ = {P̃* : dP̃*/dP* = S¹/𝔼*[S¹]
for some P* ∈ 𝒫}.

Attainable payoffs · Law of one price

𝒱 ≔ {ξ̄·S̄ : ξ̄ ∈ ℝd+1}.

Under NA,

ξ̄·S̄ = ζ̄·S̄ P-a.s. ⇒ π̄·ξ̄ = π̄·ζ̄.

Define the price of V ∈ 𝒱:

π(V) ≔ π̄·ξ̄   when V = ξ̄·S̄.

Remark. π is a linear form on the finite-dimensional space 𝒱. Each P* ∈ 𝒫 gives a linear extension to L¹(P*) via

π(V) = 𝔼*[V/(1+r)],   V ∈ 𝒱.

These extensions need not be unique.

Definition · Nonredundancy

ξ̄·S̄ = 0 P-a.s. ⇒ ξ̄ = 0.

Remark. Otherwise, under NA, one asset is a linear combination of the others in both payoff and price; remove it.

Lemma · One price does not imply no arbitrage

The law of one price does not imply NA.

Example: r=0, π¹=1, S¹∈{1,2}, both states possible. Payoff vectors are independent, but ξ̄=(−1,1) costs 0 and pays (0,1).

Return · Definition & combination rule

R(V) ≔ (V−π(V))/π(V),   π(V) ≠ 0.

For V = ∑k αkVk, with nonzero relevant prices,

R(V) = ∑k βkR(Vk),
βk = αkπ(Vk)/π(V),   ∑kβk = 1.

βk is the fraction of the investment in Vk.

Proposition · Expected returns

Under NA, for V ∈ 𝒱 with π(V) ≠ 0:

(a)   𝔼*[R(V)] = r.
(b)   𝔼Q[R(V)] = r − CovQ(dP*/dQ, R(V)).

In (a), P* is any risk-neutral measure. In (b), Q ∼ P, 𝔼Q|S| < ∞, and P* ∈ 𝒫.

NA means no arbitrage. Barred symbols include the bond; unbarred vectors contain risky assets. All payoff comparisons are P-a.s.
