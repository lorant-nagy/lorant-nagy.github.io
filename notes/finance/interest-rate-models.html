Interest rates

Definitions & connections · basic default-free, single-curve setup

Notation: t = observation date; T = maturity date; h = T − t > 0 = duration in years.

1 · Return & interest conventions

Invest X; receive X(1 + a(h)) after h years. For this section, the starting date is implicit.

Relative return

Gain relative to the initial investment; not annualized.

a(h) = final value − XX
Annualized simple rate

Expresses the whole-period return proportionally to duration.

r(h) = a(h)h   ⇔   1 + a(h) = 1 + r(h)h
Continuously compounded rate

Equivalent constant exponential growth rate.

y(h) = log(1 + a(h))h   ⇔   1 + a(h) = ey(h)h

These are alternative quotes for the same period’s return. “Annualized” needs a convention; one maturity’s quote does not determine another. Continuous compounding requires a positive final value. The symbols a(h), r(h) and y(h) are our introductory shorthand.

Instantaneous rate:   k(t) = limh↓0 a(t,h)h;   dSt = k(t)St dt.

Here a(t,h) is the local relative return starting at t, for differentiable accumulation. A fixed simple rate gives 1 + rh; a fixed continuous rate gives eyh.

2 · Bonds, yields & forward rates

Throughout, t ≤ T₁ < T₂ and δ = T₂ − T₁ is measured in years. Forward-contract cash flows are from the lender’s perspective. No money changes hands at t; the quoted parameter is chosen so that the agreement has zero value when entered. Assume positive bond prices and the required maturity derivatives and finite limits exist.

2.1 · Definitions

Zero-coupon (discount) bond
P(t,T)

Price at t of a bond paying 1 at T, with no intermediate payments. It is also the discount factor for that maturity.

P(T,T) = 1.
Forward bond price
P(t;T₁,T₂)		Contract agreed at t: pay q at T₁; receive 1 at T₂. The forward bond price is the amount q making this agreement worth zero at t.
−qP(t,T₁) + P(t,T₂) = 0,   q := P(t;T₁,T₂).
Continuously compounded
forward yield
y(t;T₁,T₂)		Contract agreed at t: lend 1 at T₁; receive eδc at T₂. The forward yield is the annualized continuous rate c making this agreement worth zero at t.
−P(t,T₁) + eδcP(t,T₂) = 0,   c := y(t;T₁,T₂).
Zero-coupon (spot) yield
y(t;t,T)

The immediately starting case of the forward yield. At fixed observation date t, the function T ↦ y(t;t,T) is the zero-coupon yield curve.

Annualized simple forward rate
L(t;T₁,T₂)		Contract agreed at t: lend 1 at T₁; receive 1 + δℓ at T₂. The simple forward rate is the annualized rate ℓ making this agreement worth zero at t.
−P(t,T₁) + (1 + δℓ)P(t,T₂) = 0,   ℓ := L(t;T₁,T₂).
Instantaneous forward rate
f(t,T)

Limit of the continuously compounded forward yield as the lending period shrinks, with observation date t and future starting date T fixed.

f(t,T) := limδ↓0 y(t;T,T+δ).
Short rate
rt

Instantaneous rate for investing at the observation date; the continuously reinvested money market account earns this rate.

rt := f(t,t).

2.2 · Basic relations

Finite-period quantities from bond prices.

P(t;T₁,T₂) = P(t,T₂)P(t,T₁),   L(t;T₁,T₂) = P(t,T₁)/P(t,T₂) − 1δ,
y(t;T₁,T₂) = log P(t,T₁) − log P(t,T₂)δ.

Conversion between conventions.

P(t;T₁,T₂) = [1 + δL(t;T₁,T₂)]−1 = e−δy(t;T₁,T₂).

Spot yield and bond price.

P(t,T) = e−y(t;t,T)(T−t),   y(t;t,T) = −log P(t,T)T − t.

Maturity derivative and accumulation. The observation date t is held fixed.

f(t,T) = −∂Tlog P(t,T),   P(t,T) = exp(−∫tT f(t,u)du).
f(t,T) = y(t;t,T) + (T − t)∂Ty(t;t,T).

Money market account.

dBt = rtBtdt.

2.3 · Properties

Common instantaneous limit. The simple forward rate has the same limiting value as the continuously compounded yield used to define f:

limδ↓0 L(t;T,T+δ) = f(t,T).

Indeed, the conversion identity above gives L = (eδy − 1)/δ. Since eδx = 1 + δx + o(δ) for bounded x as δ → 0, the simple and continuous conventions agree to first order.

Yield as an average across maturities.

y(t;t,T) = 1T − t ∫tT f(t,u)du.

This averages the forward curve observed at t across future maturities, not future realized short rates.

Short-maturity limit of the spot yield.

rt = limh↓0 y(t;t,t+h).

For an immediately starting bond investment with h = T − t, our earlier annualized simple rate r(h) equals L(t;t,t+h). It is distinct from the instantaneous short-rate notation rt.

3 · Short-rate modelling: general setup

Pricing relation. Let P(t,T) be the price of a zero-coupon bond with payoff P(T,T) = 1, and let rt be the adapted short rate. Define

dBt = rtBtdt,   B0 = 1,   Bt = exp(∫0t rsds).

Under the standard no-arbitrage pricing assumptions, let Q be an equivalent pricing measure with numeraire B. Assume the discounted bond prices are true Q-martingales. Consequently,

P(t,T)/Bt = EQ[P(T,T)/BT | ℱt] = EQ[1/BT | ℱt],
P(t,T) = EQ[exp(−∫tT rsds) | ℱt].

Model specification. Specify the short-rate dynamics directly under the pricing measure:

drt = μt(ϑ)dt + σt(ϑ) · dWtQ,   r0 given,

where WQ is a Q-Brownian motion. The adapted coefficients may depend on auxiliary states, including stochastic variance. Under the required admissibility and integrability conditions, this specification implies model bond prices

Pϑ(t,T) = EQ[exp(−∫tT rs(ϑ)ds) | ℱt].

The pricing relation enforces internal no-arbitrage consistency; it does not ensure agreement with observed prices.

Calibration. Given the observed initial curve Pobs(0,T), choose ϑ so that

Pϑ(0,T) = Pobs(0,T)   for the calibration maturities,

if an exact fit is attainable; otherwise seek an approximate fit. Calibration selects among internally consistent models to match the market. It is not, in general, the procedure that makes the model arbitrage-free. Option prices impose further constraints on volatility and dependence.

Examples

Vasicek
drt = κ(θ − rt)dt + σdWtQ
CIR
drt = κ(θ − rt)dt + σ√rtdWtQ
Hull–White
drt = [b(t) − κrt]dt + σdWtQ (Can fit the entire initial market bond-price curve exactly.)
Fong–Vasicek
Stochastic variance
drt = κr(θr − rt)dt + √vtdW1,tQ
dvt = κv(θv − vt)dt + ξ√vtdW2,tQ
d⟨W1Q,W2Q⟩t = ρdt

Fong–Vasicek has two state variables, r and v; κr,κv,θv,ξ > 0, v0 ≥ 0 and |ρ| ≤ 1. Its random variance is spanned by the bond curve. The book distinguishes this from unspanned stochastic volatility, which cannot be recovered from the contemporaneous bond curve.

κ,σ > 0; CIR uses θ > 0 and r0 ≥ 0. In Hull–White, b(t) is deterministic and chosen to fit the initial curve.

4 · Instantaneous forward-rate modelling: the HJM framework

Pricing relation. The instantaneous forward rates and bond prices satisfy

f(t,T) = −∂Tlog P(t,T),   P(t,T) = exp(−∫tT f(t,u)du),   rt = f(t,t).

Let Q be a pricing measure with the money market account B as numeraire. In a Brownian setting, consider dynamics under Q of the form

df(t,T) = α(t,T)dt + σf(t,T) · dWtQ,

where WQ is a vector-valued Q-Brownian motion, σf is an adapted volatility vector, and a dot denotes the Euclidean inner product. Define

Σ(t,T) = ∫tT σf(t,u)du.

No-arbitrage restriction. Under sufficient regularity, Itô’s formula gives

dP(t,T)/P(t,T) = [rt − ∫tT α(t,u)du + ½‖Σ(t,T)‖²]dt − Σ(t,T) · dWtQ.

The no-arbitrage condition concerns the discounted bond price. Since dBₜ/Bₜ = rₜdt, its dynamics are

d(P(t,T)/Bt)/(P(t,T)/Bt) = [−∫tT α(t,u)du + ½‖Σ(t,T)‖²]dt − Σ(t,T) · dWtQ.

For P(t,T)/Bt to be a local Q-martingale, this drift must be zero. Equivalently, the undiscounted bond’s relative drift equals rt. Hence

∫tT α(t,u)du = ½‖Σ(t,T)‖².

Differentiating with respect to T yields the HJM drift condition:

α(t,T) = σf(t,T) · ∫tT σf(t,u)du.

Thus an admissible volatility specification determines the drift under Q. The resulting bond dynamics are

dP(t,T)/P(t,T) = rtdt − Σ(t,T) · dWtQ.

These statements require the usual existence and regularity conditions; additional integrability is required when true, rather than local, martingales are needed.

Initial curve and calibration. Use the observed initial curve as input:

f(0,T) = −∂Tlog Pobs(0,T).

For a positive, sufficiently smooth initial discount curve with Pobs(0,0) = 1, this matches initial bond prices by construction. Volatility parameters may then be calibrated to option prices. The initial curve does not determine the volatility specification.

Examples

For one Brownian driver, let τ = T − t, with κ,σ > 0.

Ho–Lee / constant forward volatility
σf(t,T) = σ,   α(t,T) = σ²τ.
Gaussian / Hull–White
σf(t,T) = σe−κτ,
α(t,T) = (σ²/κ)e−κτ(1 − e−κτ).
Separable stochastic-volatility extension
σf(t,T) = ηte−κτ,
α(t,T) = (ηt²/κ)e−κτ(1 − e−κτ).

In the last example, η is an adapted volatility process whose dynamics and joint dependence on the rate drivers must also be specified. Chapter 13 develops local- and stochastic-volatility quasi-Gaussian specifications of this type. The Gaussian example has a Hull–White short-rate representation; the viewpoints are not disjoint model classes.

5 · LIBOR market models: finite-period forward rates

Starting point: fix a tenor grid T0 < ⋯ < TN. For each period define δi = Ti+1 − Ti and the rate below, modelled up to its fixing date Ti.

Li(t) = L(t;Ti,Ti+1) = P(t,Ti)/P(t,Ti+1) − 1δi
P(t,Tm)/P(t,Tk) = ∏i=km−1 [1 + δiLi(t)]−1,   t ≤ Tk

dLi(t) = bi(t) · dWtQᵀⁱ⁺¹

5.2 · Classical lognormal LMM / BGM

bi(t) = Li(t)λi(t),   λi(t) deterministic

5.3 · Joint dynamics under the terminal measure

To evolve all unfixed rates together, choose one measure, for example QTᴺ, whose numeraire is P(t,TN). For a general vector diffusion coefficient:

dLi = μi dt + bi · dWtQᵀᴺ,   μi = −∑j=i+1N−1 δj bi · bj1 + δjLj
d⟨Li,Lj⟩t = bi · bj dt

5.4 · Local and stochastic volatility extensions

Lognormal
bi = Liλi
Displaced lognormal
bi = (Li + si)λi
CEV-type
bi = Liβλi,   0 < β < 1
Common stochastic variance
bi = √Vt gi(Li)λi

For example, under the terminal measure one may use an independent square-root variance process:

dVt = κv(θv − Vt)dt + ξ√Vt dZt,   Z independent of WQᵀᴺ,   κv,θv,ξ > 0

Admissibility matters: 1 + δiLi must remain positive. For a positive shift, si < 1/δi is a sufficient restriction in the displaced-lognormal setup. CEV needs a boundary convention at zero. An unrestricted additive Gaussian (“normal”) rate can cross the bond-price boundary, so it is not automatically a globally valid LMM extension.

5.6 · SABR: a related single-rate model

For one selected forward rate F, under its appropriate forward measure (or, for a swap rate, its swap measure, using the swap annuity as numeraire), a standard SABR specification is:

dFt = αtFtβ dWt,   dαt = ναtdZt,   d⟨W,Z⟩t = ρ dt

α0 > 0 is initial volatility; ν ≥ 0 controls its randomness; β controls rate dependence; −1 ≤ ρ ≤ 1 links rate and volatility shocks. The book’s CEV treatment uses 0 < β < 1; lognormal and normal cases are familiar limiting choices, with their own domain considerations.

The book also distinguishes the SABR stochastic model from approximate smile formulas: an approximation is not automatically arbitrage-free, and one constant parameter set need not fit every option maturity.
